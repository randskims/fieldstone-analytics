The Competent Token: Composing a Free Competence Gate with Integrity and Provenance, and the Trust-Domain Boundary of a Deterministic Edge Decision

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 25, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24546260

Abstract

A frozen, deterministic, class-discriminant single-token decision encoder reduces a window of sensor observations to one 8-bit token by a fixed feature front end, a collapsed-affine projection, and a nearest-of-256-or-fewer-centroid lookup. Companion studies showed this determinism makes a logged decision checkable (a self-checking token) and provable (a verifiable-provenance token closed by a keyed anchor). This work adds the third question a deployed decision must answer -- "should this decision be trusted at all, or is its input outside the commissioned envelope?" -- and asks whether the competence gate that answers it (the nearest-centroid distance, read off the same embedding whose index is the token) composes with the integrity and provenance readouts off one committed window, cheaply, cleanly, and with an honest boundary. It does, and the boundary is the point.
