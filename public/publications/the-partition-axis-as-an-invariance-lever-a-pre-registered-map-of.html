The Partition Axis as an Invariance Lever: A Pre-Registered Map of Recalibration-Free Amplitude-Drift Robustness in a Frozen Decision Token

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 10, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24120986

Abstract

A frozen, deterministic, class-discriminant encoder reduces each window of a multivariate stream to a single 8-bit decision token by a fixed pipeline (a no-learned-parameter feature front-end → a supervised linear-discriminant ⊕ principal-component subspace → a k-means codebook of at most 256 cells → a nearest-centroid index). The front-end is a free choice: one may build the token on different partition axes — different fixed views of the signal. We ask, under pre-registered protocols with frozen outcome bands, what the choice of axis buys. Across twenty-three pre-registered tiers on 108 UCR/UEA datasets plus raw industrial vibration (CWRU, MFPT), wearable inertial (HAPT), twelve-lead clinical electrocardiography (PTB-XL), and industrial machine audio (MIMII), we find that the partition axis is an invariance lever: a relational axis — ordinal (rank patterns), recurrence (recurrence-quantification), or topological (persistence) — is 7–15× more robust to amplitude- and gain-class drift than the per-channel axes (raw-channel statistics, spectral bands): under a monotone nonlinear gain the channel token loses 0.275 macro-AUROC (95% CI [0.246, 0.305]), collapsing from 0.811 to 0.535, while an ordinal token loses 0.001, recurrence 0.023, and topological 0.036 (the spectral-band axis is intermediate, losing 0.229). This invariance is, for the relational axes, substantially a property of their rank- or scale-normalized construction; the paper's contribution is the quantified accuracy, cost, calibration, and bound map that follows, and the localization of the effect to one readout class. The advantage is not an input-normalization artifact (it beats a z-normalized channel baseline by +0.252 under nonlinear gain), is hyperparameter-robust, leakage-free, and drift-model-general (holding across per-channel gain, DC offset, companding, and clipping), and leaves the token better-calibrated under drift. It beats memoryless recalibration (a relational token scores 0.80 under nonlinear gain — the single topological axis, 0.766, already suffices — where a translational recalibration of the channel token, which cannot by construction undo a nonlinear gain, scores 0.55) and training-time augmentation (relational wins on 80% of datasets), shows a modest, amplitude-dominated advantage under real cross-condition drift (+0.140 across recording-condition sibling pairs), and holds on eleven of fifteen tested domains spanning clinical ECG, industrial vibration, and machine audio. We characterize the bounds with equal care: it is not an accuracy lever (composition of axes adds only generic multi-token diversity), not a boundary lever (it does not cross the EEG/MEG relational boundary), not robust to additive noise or bit-quantization, and its large magnitude is specific to the linear/lookup readout class the frozen token belongs to — a readout-capacity dial shows the advantage is ~0.23 for the token and a linear classifier alike and collapses to ~0.04 the instant a nonlinear tree readout is introduced. A deployment map follows: choose ordinal when cost-constrained (≈2× the channel front-end on a per-window compute proxy, at a bounded accuracy tax) and topological when accuracy is paramount (near-free accuracy, ≈80× compute). This is a characterization of previously described, filed methods; it discloses no new algorithmic subject matter. The general decision rule is disclosed; the quantitative per-deployment drift-diagnostic and commissioning thresholds are retained as trade secret.
