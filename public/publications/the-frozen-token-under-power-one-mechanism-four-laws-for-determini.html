The Frozen Token Under Power: One Mechanism, Four Laws for Deterministic Edge Inference at the Energy Boundary

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 24, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24513981

Abstract

A frozen, deterministic, class-discriminant single-token decision encoder reduces a window of sensor observations to one 8-bit token by a fixed feature front end, a collapsed-affine projection, and a nearest-of-256-centroid lookup. A four-part line of work established, separately, that this substrate survives intermittent power bit-exact, adapts under power loss deterministically, runs many always-on capabilities for nearly the cost of one, and self-checks its own integrity for free. This paper's thesis is that these are one fact wearing four hats — the per-window decision is a bounded pure function of a committed, bounded input window, carrying zero progress state — and that each property is a corollary: survival is re-executing the pure function from the committed window; adaptation is re-deriving a bounded sufficient statistic (a mean) from that same committed ring (power-failure-atomic exactly when the statistic is bounded, which holds exactly when the drift is translational); composition is sharing the one feature front end the pure function already computes (sub-additive when shared, additive when not); and self-check is recomputing the decision and comparing it to a retained fingerprint (free against accidental corruption, blind to a consistency-preserving adversary). The unification is made falsifiable and tested: it predicts that every perturbation acts on the decision only through one scalar — the displacement of the committed window's embedding — and, reparametrized by that scalar, four mechanically different perturbation families (noise, quantization, truncation, drift) collapse onto one response curve, shrinking the cross-mechanism spread 3.8× in decision-flip and 8.1× in monitor margin versus a raw-strength baseline. The new empirical contribution is the joint keystone — the one experiment the four source papers never ran — all four properties composed into a single firmware image, off one committed ring and one shared front end, measured on real silicon under true power cuts, including on a modality held out of every source run: the composed stack's determinism digest is bit-exact across two commodity microcontrollers (a Cortex-M33 metered with a Power Profiler Kit II and a Cortex-M4F), across 1,000 true power-cut cold boots, and across three compiler optimization levels; composed energy is a measured 1.23× the base decision (four always-on capabilities for about 13 µJ); the adaptation re-derives bit-exact under any cut timing; and the self-check catches every decision-changing random fault at one comparison and no measurable energy while remaining, by design, blind to a consistency-preserving tamper. The economics are reported honestly in both directions: against a linear readout on the same features the token ties on energy (both front-end-dominated), so its edge is the invariant package it delivers at that energy — a one-byte composable substrate, a free label-free monitor, a self-check fingerprint, and thousands-fold transmission compression; against the neural frontier at accuracy parity the token runs an order of magnitude fewer operations, a claim hardened from a count into a direct on-silicon cycle measurement (49× the token base, exceeding the 26× operation-count floor); survival buys a 65× reduction in hold-up capacitance and about 126 million composed decisions per coin cell; and the only per-power-cycle cost is a platform cold-wake that a clock-configuration line cuts ~13× (about 553 ms to about 41 ms). Every load-bearing number is audited against an independent reference before deposit. The whole result is a synthesis and characterization of previously published, separately patent-pending matter; it introduces no new claimed mechanism, and the per-deployment provisioning selection is retained as trade secret.
