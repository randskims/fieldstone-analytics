The Frozen Token Reaches Quantum: a complete, pre-registered map of a deterministic single-token encoder's monitoring laws on a real quantum processor — fingerprint, privacy, integrity, qubit health, drift, and a molecular-state twin Randolph James Ferlic, M.D. and Kimberly Kate Ferlic — Fieldstone Analytics, LLC, Austin, TX, USA Preprint · Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23670079 · CC-BY 4.0 · Community: spiral-domain-encoder-campaign · This record characterizes previously filed and published methods applied to a new signal source (a quantum processor's measurement outputs) and discloses no new patentable subject matter; the one candidate new application (client-side quantum-job attestation) was deliberately not pursued as intellectual property — see the notice below. Abstract A frozen, deterministic single-token encoder reduces a window of inputs, by a fixed computation, to one 8-bit token and a bounded ≤256-cell lookup, with the nearest-centroid distance serving as a label-free novelty score. A prior program established, across many classical sensing modalities, a predictive theory of this encoder — a competence-zone separability law, a per-entity self-twin law, a compose-versus-recommission law, a cost-not-accuracy law, a deployment-sizing law, a privacy-inversion bound, a safety-interlock characterization, and a geometry-gated drift-adaptation capability. This record asks whether those laws extend to a new kind of entity: a real cloud quantum processor, probed only through the data a present-day gate-model machine returns — measurement counts (single-shot in-phase/quadrature readout is no longer exposed). Established by a pre-registration with frozen falsifiable bands and a simulate-then-validate-on-hardware discipline, the answer is that the monitoring laws reach a quantum processor essentially in full, from counts alone, with no new algorithm. A frozen 1-byte token fingerprints which of three IBM Heron-r2 156-qubit backends ran a job (balanced accuracy 1.00; chance 0.333), proves the signature stable rather than shot-noise (same-device test-retest 0.65 ≪ 1.00), exposes a privacy-inversion (device re-identification 1.00 under an abstracted backend), acts as a result-integrity dead-switch (wrong-device 1.00; gross tamper ≥1σ 0.98–1.00, including a real circuit-level tamper at 1.00 with the novelty spiking ~95× and no cheap null-space evasion; honest subtle-tamper blind spot 0.51), recovers a device's qubit-level calibration ranking from a four-second probe (Spearman 0.88; eight of ten worst qubits; generalizing to a second device at 0.86), and builds a molecular-state twin (H₂ on real hardware: bond-distortion Spearman 1.00; energy ordering 0.771; distortion-twin robust across a finer 12-geometry ladder and two Heron devices, 0.96–0.99). The keystone laws transfer: the separability screen forecasts reach when matched to the monitor's geometry (matched 1.00; generic 0.69, a characterized blind spot); cost-not-accuracy holds (byte = 200-tree forest, Δ0.00); sizing holds (≈4 commissioning sub-batches for fingerprinting, ≈8 for integrity); compose-versus-recommission holds exactly and closes a clean division of labor with the drift law. A hybrid classical→quantum→LLM pipeline monitor localizes which stage faulted at 100% for three bytes. Temporal drift (QF-2) is positive and tracks the device's calibration state rather than the clock — over seven snapshots across nine days the token's label-free novelty tracks IBM's own reported readout error at Spearman +0.89, more tightly than elapsed time (+0.68), because the device recalibrates ~daily so calibration drift is recal-cyclic (the novelty climbs and then falls back as the device is recalibrated, and the token follows it up and back down) and it tracks readout-layer drift specifically, not gate drift (+0.64), C10 reaching the quantum service-life axis, honestly scoped as a bounded n=7 result. Honest negatives are reported with equal weight (no untrained quantum-feature-map advantage, no label-derivative "synergy," no cheap two-qubit-gate-error readout). The transferable lesson: the cheapest possible deterministic monitor — one frozen byte — reaches the most advanced computing hardware we have, as a fingerprint, a privacy probe, an integrity dead-switch, and a health and drift gauge, from the measurement counts the machine already returns, without a new algorithm or a gradient. Highlights · The monitoring laws reach a real quantum processor from measurement counts alone — no new algorithm. A frozen 1-byte token fingerprints which of three IBM Heron-r2 backends ran a job (1.00; chance 0.333) and the test-retest control (same device 0.65 ≪ cross-device 1.00) proves it is a stable device signature, not shot noise. · A privacy inversion on cloud quantum. An "anonymized" backend is re-identifiable from job counts (QF-4, re-identification 1.00) — extending the program's confidentiality-is-not-anonymity bound to a quantum processor; dual-use (user-side audit / provider side-channel). · A result-integrity dead-switch with a declared blind spot. From counts, label-free: trips on a wrong/substituted device (1.00) and on gross result tampering (≥1σ, 0.98–1.00, clean dose-response) — including a real circuit-level tamper (an X-flip of half the register before measurement) at 1.00 with the novelty spiking ~95×, with no cheap null-space evasion (an adaptive quiet-direction override is caught immediately, 1.00) and no false trip on a genuine same-device re-run (0.50) — and honestly fails on subtle device-consistent (on-manifold) tampering (0.51) — defense-in-depth, not a cryptographic verifier. · Qubit-level health from a four-second probe. The token recovers the vendor's own readout-error calibration ranking (Spearman 0.88, 156 qubits) and flags eight of its ten worst qubits — a cheap, fresh, label-free health map that generalizes to a second Heron device (Spearman 0.86 at matched shots). · A molecular-state digital twin on real hardware. Commissioned on H₂'s equilibrium geometry, the token's label-free novelty tracks bond distortion (Spearman 1.00) and preserves the dissociation-energy ordering (0.771), on the device's noisy prepared state — the filed self-twin on a quantum-chemistry target; the distortion-twin is robust across a finer 12-geometry ladder and two Heron devices (0.96–0.99), its finer energy recovery shot-limited rather than device-limited. · The keystone laws transfer. The separability screen forecasts monitor reach iff matched to the monitor's geometry (matched 1.00; a generic two-class screen 0.69, blind to additive tamper); cost-not-accuracy holds (byte equals a 200-tree forest, Δ0.00); sizing holds (≈4/≈8 commissioning sub-batches); and compose-versus-recommission holds exactly — a device-A monitor flags a healthy device B at 100% and must be recommissioned, closing a clean division of labor with the drift law (small within-device drift is the drift law's regime; large cross-device shift is recommission's). · A three-byte hybrid-pipeline monitor. An array of 1-byte self-twins localizes which stage of a classical→quantum→LLM pipeline faulted at 100%, provided each stage is probed with the matched geometry (workload self-twin for covariate faults; a fixed reference probe for the quantum stage). · Honest negatives, and no new intellectual property. An untrained quantum feature-map carries no classical-data advantage, stacking label-derivative codebooks yields no super-additive "synergy," and a cheap echo probe cannot rank small two-qubit gate errors from counts. Every result is the filed, published self-twin applied to a new signal source; the one candidate new application (client-side quantum-job attestation) was weighed as intellectual property and deliberately not pursued, the chosen protection being this publication. · Trust in a cloud the user cannot see, at near-zero cost. The self-twin triad answers the practical cloud-quantum questions — did my job run on the device I was allocated (QF-1), is the abstracted backend unlinkable (QF-4; no), was my result substituted or grossly corrupted (QF-5), which qubits were healthy at run time (QF-6) — from the measurement histogram the user already receives, with no provider cooperation, trusted server, or extra circuits. The provider-side alternatives (randomized benchmarking, tomography, cryptographic verification) spend many designed circuits, privileged access, or interaction; this is the "read the receipt you already have" layer, and it is defense-in-depth beneath verification, not a replacement for it. · One primitive, more than twenty orders of magnitude. The identical mechanism now monitors a vibrating bearing, an electrocardiogram, a radio emitter, an industrial audio machine, and the measurement readout of a 156-qubit superconducting processor — a single cheap, deterministic, auditable primitive reaching the exotic end of the computing-substrate spectrum, the strongest evidence yet for the program's cost-not-accuracy premise (see the breadth figure). · Temporal drift (QF-2) is positive and vendor-corroborated — the token tracks the device's calibration *state*, not the clock. Over seven snapshots across nine days and six recalibration cycles, the token's label-free novelty tracks IBM's own historical reported readout error at Spearman +0.89 — more tightly than elapsed time (+0.68) — and the two-qubit gate error at only +0.64. Because the device recalibrates ~daily, calibration drift is recal-cyclic (the novelty rises, then is recalibrated back toward baseline), and the token follows the calibration state up and back down: the two late snapshots have low novelty and low reported readout error even though their gate error is the worst of the series. C10 on the quantum service-life axis, honestly bounded at n=7. The transferable lesson A single, auditable, sub-milliwatt monitoring primitive — one frozen deterministic byte — now demonstrably spans classical sensing and a real quantum processor, read through nothing but the measurement histogram the machine already returns. The value is not difficulty (a heavier model would also distinguish three machines); it is that the cheapest possible monitor does it, and does it the same way it monitors a bearing or an electrocardiogram: as a fingerprint, a privacy probe, an integrity dead-switch, and a health and drift gauge. The reach is maximally portable precisely because it needs only counts, and it composes with the platform's other capabilities on one runtime. The methodological corollary travels with it: validate in simulation before spending scarce hardware, build on the data the machine actually returns (measurement counts) so the monitor ships on the hardware that exists now, and characterize the reach with a geometry-matched separability screen rather than assuming it — and draw the map's negatives (no quantum-feature-map advantage, no stacking synergy, no cheap gate-error readout), because a bounded capability that knows its own blind spot is worth more than an over-claimed one.
