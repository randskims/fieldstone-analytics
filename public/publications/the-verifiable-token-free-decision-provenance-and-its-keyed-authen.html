The Verifiable Token: Free Decision Provenance and Its Keyed Authenticity Boundary for Edge Inference

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 25, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24540844

Abstract

A frozen, deterministic, class-discriminant single-token decision encoder reduces a window of sensor observations to one 8-bit token by a fixed feature front end, a collapsed-affine projection, and a nearest-of-≤256-centroid lookup. This work asks what that determinism buys for the trust of an edge decision — can a third party verify that a logged decision was correctly produced by a stated model on a stated input, and where is the line between a decision one can verify was computed correctly and one can prove is authentic — and answers with a sharp, measured boundary. Because the model is public and the decision is a bit-exact pure function of the committed window, reproducibility is free: an independent verifier using only the public model re-derives the producer's token at 100.000% agreement with zero disagreements across seven sensor modalities (8,528 windows). From it follow tamper-evidence (altering a logged decision is caught by re-derivation at detection 1.000 on every modality) and auditability (a hash-chained log whose three verifiers — free re-derivation, free chain, keyed tag — each catch a distinct tampering class with zero interference on a clean log). But the same public determinism makes decision-authenticity forgeable: a chosen decision is forged in closed form, one shot, independent of its 5.8–6.2 bits of entropy (forge rate 1.00), so un-attested fabrication-detection is 0.00; a keyed commitment binding the committed input, a nonce, and the device identity closes it (1.00 detection, 0.00 genuine false-reject). The whole provenance check — one re-derivation plus one keyed tag verify — is 1.3–1.7× a single inference on two commodity microcontrollers and bit-exact across both cores. The decisive reproducibility result is cross-architecture: a device's ARM-CMSIS float32 token equals a host's x86-numpy float64 token bit-for-bit — 0 mismatches across 128 windows, two cores, two modalities, different instruction set, precision, and FFT — so third-party verifiability is demonstrated, not asserted. A boundary-margin census shows the determinism is structural, not lucky (0 of 8,528 samples within the floating-point danger zone of a boundary; 0 token flips under single precision, reduced reduction order, or emulated fused multiply-add). The work introduces no new claimed mechanism; the cryptographic primitives are textbook; and the per-deployment provisioning selection is retained as a trade secret.
