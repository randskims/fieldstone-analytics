The Constellation Boundary: An Out-of-Sample RF Test of the Frozen-Token Predictive Theory on Automatic Modulation Classification Randolph James Ferlic, M.D. and Kimberly Kate Ferlic — Fieldstone Analytics, LLC, Austin, TX, USA Preprint · Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23003249 · CC-BY 4.0 · Community: spiral-domain-encoder-campaign · a characterization of previously filed and published methods; no new algorithmic subject matter is disclosed. Abstract A companion capstone proposed a predictive theory of a frozen single-token encoder as an always-on Tier-0 sensing layer — four falsifiable laws — and validated it out-of-sample on a chemical e-nose, while pre-registering a second out-of-sample test on RF automatic modulation classification that was deferred because the corpus was registration-gated. Having located a public mirror, we run it here. On RadioML 2016.10a (11 modulations × 20 SNRs × ~1000 examples; 2×128 IQ), with a fixed 128-dim no-learned-parameters RF front-end (instantaneous amplitude/phase/frequency statistics, higher-order cumulants, spectral bands), we test the six predictions the capstone registered before any RF data existed, and confirm all six. The token is near-parity on a few-class high-SNR subset (0.970 vs 0.992) and pays a real priced tax on the full 11-class task (0.726 vs 0.853) — a tax, not a low ceiling. Most sharply, per-modulation recall splits along the constellation boundary the theory predicts: the token wins on modulations separable by spectral/amplitude/frequency structure (AM-SSB 1.00, CPFSK/GFSK 0.96–0.98, PAM4/BPSK 0.86–0.93) and pays most on those separable only by fine constellation geometry (QAM16 0.35, QAM64 0.49, 8-PSK 0.46, QPSK 0.67). The confusion structure exposes the mechanism: errors concentrate on the same-family constellation sibling (QAM16↔QAM64, 8-PSK↔QPSK) — the token cannot resolve constellation order. Accuracy degrades gracefully with SNR, a high-SNR codebook re-commissions per SNR, a self-twin flags an unseen modulation at AUROC 0.941, and the near-parity survives a permutation null, a bootstrap interval, an MLP comparator, and a quantization ablation. Two independent out-of-sample validations — a chemical e-nose and now RF, the latter confirming the theory's sharpest per-class prediction — establish that the frozen token's behavior is predictable from the structure of a task. This is a characterization of previously described, filed methods; it discloses no new algorithmic subject matter, and the per-deployment / per-sensor selection of configuration is retained as trade secret. Highlights · 6/6 pre-registered predictions confirmed on RF (RadioML 2016.10a), predictions frozen in the "The Frozen Token" capstone before any RF data existed. · The constellation boundary — the token wins on spectral/amplitude/frequency-separable modulations and pays most on the high-order QAM/PSK constellations, exactly as the per-channel-vs-relational law predicts. · A demonstrated mechanism, not a correlation — errors concentrate on the same-family sibling (QAM16↔QAM64 0.46, 8-PSK↔QPSK 0.33): the token cannot resolve constellation order. · Reviewer-hardened — permutation null at chance (no leakage), tight bootstrap tax CI [0.099, 0.121], near-parity vs an MLP too, quantization ~free; plus capability composition, a codebook-size sweep, multiplexing, and a gate operating point. · A second independent out-of-sample validation of the four laws, after the e-nose — across two different sensing physics (chemical and electromagnetic).
