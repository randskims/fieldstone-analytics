The Decision Token as a Semantic Channel Code: A Frozen One-Byte Decision Primitive for Goal-Oriented Communication, Digital-Twin Synchronization, and Bit-Exact On-Device Decoding

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 6, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24020141

Abstract

Goal-oriented ("semantic") communication transmits only the information a receiver needs to act, rather than reconstructing the source waveform. We study a deliberately minimal realization of this idea and push it as hard as an adversarial communications reviewer and a hard-nosed technical evaluator would: a frozen, deterministic, ≤256-cell decision token — an offline-trained nearest-centroid codebook over a collapsed linear-discriminant/principal-component embedding, read out by a per-cell class posterior — treated as a semantic channel code that carries a single byte across a noisy link. Unlike the learned, heavy, non-deterministic encoders that dominate the field, this encoder is bit-exact, sub-milliwatt, and auditable by construction; the scientific question is how far such a primitive can be pushed as a communication object, and exactly where it stops. Across a pre-registered battery of twenty-one channel tiers (SC-1 … SC-21) and four twin-synchronization tiers (TW-1 … TW-4) on the public UCR/UEA archives plus real activity and physiological streams, with decision bands fixed before measurement and negatives kept, we establish six things. (1) The thesis holds at archive scale: transmitting the decision beats reconstruct-then-decide at a matched ~6-bit budget by +0.161 mean macro-AUROC across the full 108-dataset census (≥+0.05 on 87%, tied-or-ahead on 95%). (2) A channel-realism and streaming map: a naked token is graceful to ~1% bit-error-rate and fragile by 10%; a soft-decision lookup readout recovers much of that fragility over a real BPSK/AWGN channel (+0.048 on 8/10 at mid-SNR); and a causal, zero-latency streaming filter recovers most channel-induced error on genuinely autocorrelated streams (+0.199 at 10% BER on dwell streams; 81% of the non-causal smoother's recovery at zero latency), with a shuffle control that separates a true temporal effect from a class-prior effect. (3) An honest head-to-head with a learned joint source–channel code (Deep-JSCC), which beats the token on accuracy by +0.076 on 10/10 datasets — relocating the token's value from accuracy to determinism, bit-exactness, footprint, and auditability. (4) A security boundary: the token's geometry is not a tamper-evidence primitive (a codebook-aware adversary flips the decision with a median of one bit; cheap geometric detectors fall to 0.31 AUROC against a detector-aware attacker), so integrity must come from a system-level authentication layer — a boundary we quantify rather than paper over. (5) A digital-twin synchronization flagship: a one-byte token's control decision is unmatched by scalar-quantized full-state transmission even at 64 bits on 10/10 datasets, and a one-byte "deviation" side-channel becomes a safe competence gate under distribution shift (open-set detection 0.76 vs. 0.52 for the posterior margin; up to 0.94), while the previously disclosed drift compensator recovers a twin's own sensor drift (translational ~99%, gain ~83%) but cannot bridge two genuinely different entities without recommissioning. (6) A bit-exact reduction to practice of the receiver decode on two commodity Arm microcontrollers (0 of 400 decision flips; ~5.2 µs/symbol on a Cortex-M33 and ~13.9 µs/symbol on a Cortex-M4F; ~0.3% of the encode energy). Five pre-registered directional hypotheses were refuted and are reported as such. The study introduces no new mechanism: it re-expresses previously disclosed methods in the communication and twin-synchronization settings, and we position the "transmit-the-decision" framing itself as prior art to be cited, not claimed. The contribution is a precise, hardened, hardware-validated map of what a frozen one-byte primitive can and cannot do as a communication object — strong precisely because it names its own boundaries.
