No Winning Ticket: a Frozen Single-Token Codebook is Redundant, not a Lottery Ticket — and the pruning advantage appears only under under-provisioning Randolph James Ferlic, M.D. and Kimberly Kate Ferlic — Fieldstone Analytics, LLC, Austin, TX, USA Preprint · Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23701151 · CC-BY 4.0 · Community: spiral-domain-encoder-campaign · This record characterizes a property of a previously filed and published method (the class-discriminant single-token codebook and its deployment-sizing law); it discloses no new patentable subject matter. Abstract A frozen, deterministic single-token encoder reduces a window of inputs, by a fixed computation, to one 8-bit token indexing a bounded ≤256-cell codebook and a per-cell lookup. A prior program established a deployment-sizing law for this encoder — that the codebook size K can be driven far down ("size K down"), because one byte usually suffices. That raises a tempting question, borrowed from deep learning: is there a "winning sub-codebook" — a small, specific subset of cells, identifiable by an importance score, that carries the decision, in the sense of the lottery-ticket hypothesis? Answered here with a pre-registration and an exhaustive hardening battery, the answer is no — at the operating point. On 22 real multivariate datasets, pruning the codebook by cell importance (training occupancy × purity) does not beat random pruning at matched sparsity (mean advantage +0.0002, 95% CI [−0.012, +0.011], significantly below the +0.02 effect band, p=0.004); a steelman of seven importance metrics confirms it, and the geometrically-motivated metrics are actively counterproductive. What is true is strong redundancy: the codebook prunes to ~12–25% of its cells with ≤0.01 AUROC loss (often a small gain) by any selection method, and the pruned codebook is as robust as the full one under input noise, int8 quantization, and covariate drift. The iterative step that defines the neural lottery ticket has no analog on a frozen codebook (one-shot selection, a tie by construction). A winning ticket appears only in the under-provisioned regime (K≈64, near the sizing-law optimum), where a greedy label-optimal oracle beats the magnitude heuristic; it is clustering-independent (a Gaussian-mixture codebook reproduces it), so it is a function of codebook size relative to the task. We seal the boundary: at the operating point (K=256), even a greedy label-optimal oracle cannot find a reliable ticket (+0.0055 AUROC), and the magnitude heuristic hurts (−0.0095). The synthesis is a law with a sharp condition that unifies with the sizing law — the frozen codebook is redundant, not a lottery ticket; which cells you keep begins to matter only once the codebook is sized near the task's needs, and even then only trivially. The deployment corollary is practical and positive: ship a 4–8× smaller, equally-robust codebook lookup — and you need no clever pruning algorithm, because random works as well as importance. Highlights · No winning ticket at the operating point — statistically. Magnitude-importance pruning does not beat random at K=256 (mean +0.0002; 95% bootstrap CI [−0.012, +0.011]; t vs the +0.02 band = −3.28, p=0.004). A seven-metric steelman and a greedy label-optimal oracle both fail; the geometric and confidence metrics actively hurt. · Redundancy, not a ticket. The codebook prunes to ~12–25% of its cells with ≤0.01 AUROC loss (often a small gain) by any method — the importance ranking buys nothing. This reattributes the sizing law's "size K down" to cell interchangeability. · Pruning is free under deployment stress. Pruned-to-25% tracks the full codebook within ±0.02 (slightly better) under input noise, int8 quantization, and covariate drift — the redundant cells are not insurance — and it transfers to unseen entities. · A ticket only under under-provisioning. At a tight codebook (K≈64) a modest ticket appears and a greedy oracle beats magnitude; it is clustering-independent (Gaussian-mixture too), so it is about codebook size relative to the task, not k-means. · A practical corollary inside a negative. Ship a 4–8× smaller, equally-robust codebook lookup selected by the cheapest possible method — no importance ranking, no iterative prune-and-refit, no clever algorithm — a direct reduction in flash footprint and lookup latency at zero accuracy and zero engineering cost. The transferable lesson A pre-registered, statistically-backed, multiply-hardened negative closes a hypothesis a careful evaluator would otherwise raise — surely a smarter pruning of the codebook helps? — and turns it into a simpler, smaller deployment. The frozen token's value is its cheapness and determinism; here even the selection of its sub-codebook turns out to be free. A map with its negatives drawn — the refuted winning ticket, the counterproductive geometric metrics, the failed separability-forecast — is worth more than an over-claimed positive.
