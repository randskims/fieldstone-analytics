The Composed Platform: one pre-deployment screen predicts a multi-capability frozen-token stack under cross-domain stress — with an axis-specific reach, on commodity silicon Randolph James Ferlic, M.D. and Kimberly Kate Ferlic — Fieldstone Analytics, LLC, Austin, TX, USA Preprint · Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23401467 · CC-BY 4.0 · Community: spiral-domain-encoder-campaign · a characterization of previously filed and published methods; no new algorithmic subject matter is disclosed. Abstract A frozen, deterministic single-token encoder exposes nine composable capabilities — accuracy, robustness, and monitoring — on one runtime, and a prior program has shown that a single class-separability measurement, computed once on a deployment's commissioning data, predicts the encoder's single-capability behavior across domains. This paper answers the question a technical evaluator actually asks: does the composed stack hold under cross-domain stress, and can one cheap probe de-risk the whole platform at once? We run a pre-registered study across sixteen domains spanning seven modality families and four stressors, with five falsifiable hypotheses and frozen bands. The central finding is an honest boundary, not a blanket win. Composition is free and deterministic. Across ninety-six capability-additions under stress, 100% are non-destructive (0 destructive beyond a max(0.01, seed-spread) margin), and the composed stack is bit-exact — 0 token flips on the host, anchored to 0/256 flips on two microcontroller architectures (float32 and int8). One screen predicts the composed safety gate — and its reach is axis-specific. A single commissioning-time separability measurement predicts the composed safety gate (Spearman +0.92, permutation p = 0.0002, thirteen domains), and that reach is axis-specific: the same screen is blind to corruption-robustness (Spearman −0.23, p = 0.39 — refuted exactly as pre-registered). The screen answers the accuracy, label-budget, and safety-gate questions, because those are properties of where the classes sit; it does not answer the corruption-robustness question, which is a property of how the input is degraded and needs its own input-conditioning dial. The honest mitigation limit. Label-free recentering recovers only ~30% of cross-domain loss (not a majority), so the deployment consequence is a requirement, not a defect: under drift the stack must be recalibrated per-epoch, and label-free recentering is a partial mitigation between recalibrations. It runs on commodity silicon, measured and hardened. On an Arm Cortex-M33 the frozen runtime is bit-exact (0/256 flips across two architectures, two models, and both numeric paths, replicated across twelve power-on resets); its worst-case inference latency is 1.573 ms with near-zero jitter (a spike, not a spread, 3× inside a 5 ms budget); a continuous 50,678-inference soak produced zero token changes and zero faults; per-inference energy is 9.73 ± 0.07 µJ on a sub-milliwatt idle floor; and on-device compute costs less than a single BLE advertising event (16.6 µJ, including the CPU wake and three-channel transmission), so shipping the one-byte token instead of the raw window saves roughly 20–70× in radio energy. Highlights · A composable platform, not features that fight each other: 96/96 capability-additions non-destructive under cross-domain stress; 0 token flips (0/256 on silicon). · One cheap commissioning probe predicts the composed safety gate (Spearman +0.92, p = 0.0002) — the program's separability law survives composition. · The honest boundary that makes the probe deployable: the same screen is blind to corruption-robustness (Spearman −0.23). Run the probe for the class-geometry axes; condition the input for the rest. · A stated requirement, not a hidden weakness: label-free recentering recovers only ~30% of cross-domain loss → recalibrate per-epoch under drift. · A measured, hardened silicon envelope: WCET 1.573 ms (≈0 jitter), a 50,678-inference zero-drift soak, 9.73 ± 0.07 µJ/inference, compute cheaper than one radio packet, 12.7 KB footprint, sub-milliwatt. · The economics of one byte (the deployment implications): on-device compute moves the energy bottleneck off the radio — ~25× lower energy per delivered decision and ~150–4,000× smaller transmit/store footprint, on a ~$10-class commodity microcontroller, with the raw waveform never leaving the device. · One platform, nine composable capabilities, five invariants: a deployment composes a subset on one runtime; Tier-0 on-device execution is the fifth architectural invariant (proven on silicon), and AI-safety runtime monitoring is the flagship application of the self-twin capability — not separate capabilities. · Two of five pre-registered hypotheses refuted — each refutation sharpening the claim rather than undermining it. This work characterizes previously filed and published methods and discloses no new algorithmic subject matter. The nine capabilities (soft-vote readout, token-ladder / product-quantizer, drift/noise co-channels, inference-time fusion, guarded recalibration, label-free self-twin monitor, selective-fidelity and channel-attribution) are the program's filed and deposited methods, and the separability screen is previously published; k-means and vector quantization, Fisher discriminant analysis, nearest-centroid and k-nearest-neighbor classification, random forests, product quantization, a distance-based out-of-distribution detector, a two-sample shift detector, pretrained time-series foundation models used only as comparators, and principal-component analysis are established prior art or standard tools, used only as tools or comparators. The method characterized — the class-discriminant single-token codebook encoder, its composable capabilities, and its label-free nearest-centroid novelty score (the filed self-twin / Tier-0 gate), together with the previously published separability screen — is the subject of filed and pending U.S. patent applications held by the authors, or is already published. The results reported here — that the composition is non-destructive and deterministic, that one separability screen predicts the composed safety gate while being blind to corruption-robustness, that label-free recentering recovers a minority of cross-domain loss, and that the stack runs as a bounded, deterministic, sub-milliwatt computation on commodity microcontrollers — are properties of those filed and published methods composed and stressed, measured on public data and on named development hardware, not new subject matter. The per-deployment configuration-selection and input-conditioning procedure, and the silicon productization and commissioning toolchain, are retained as trade secrets and deliberately not disclosed; the platform is characterized here with the plain labeled capabilities, the published screen, and the measured envelope. No third-party partner, customer, or commercial collaborator is named; the development kit and microcontroller named for the on-silicon measurements are a public hardware platform cited for reproducibility, not a partner.
