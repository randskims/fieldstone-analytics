The Confidential Token: Composing a Keyed Confidentiality Readout with Integrity, Provenance, and Competence off a Deterministic Edge Decision, and the Authenticity Boundary of Encryption Alone

Randolph James Ferlic & Kimberly Kate Ferlic · Preprint · Nov 27, 2026 · Zenodo

Download PDFView on Zenododoi.org/zenodo.24599616

Abstract

A frozen, deterministic, class-discriminant single-token decision encoder reduces a window of sensor observations to one eight-bit token by a fixed feature front end, a collapsed-affine projection, and a nearest-of-256-or-fewer-centroid lookup. Companion studies showed this determinism makes a logged decision checkable (a self-checking integrity token), provable (a verifiable-provenance token closed by a keyed anchor), and gated (a competence readout that answers whether a decision should be trusted at all). This work adds the fourth trust question a deployed decision raises -- "who may read it?" -- and asks whether a keyed confidentiality readout composed off the same committed window is cheap, exact for the authorized holder, opaque to an eavesdropper, and honest about its boundary. It is, and the boundary is the point: encryption alone is not authentication.
